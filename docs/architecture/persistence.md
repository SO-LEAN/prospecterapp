# Persistence

This document states the rules in force. The decision records (`docs/adr`) explain why: read them only for that, or before changing a rule (ADR 0090).

The aggregates live in DynamoDB, one table per bounded context (ADR 0038, 0048). Queries read their read models from the same tables without restoring the aggregates (ADR 0088).

## Tables and items

Each bounded context has its own DynamoDB table (ADR 0038, 0048): `<DYNAMODB_TABLE_PREFIX>-<context>` (`prospecter-dev-prospecting`; the prefix is `prospecter-test` in tests). It is a `Shared\Persistence\Table`, injected by name into its own context's persistence only (`Table $prospectingTable`). `tests/Architecture/TablePerContextTest` fails otherwise. Nothing spans two contexts except the consistency check, which reads every table and writes none (`Consistency\ContextTables`, `api.md`, ADR 0105). Locally, the tables live in MiniStack (the `ministack` service in `docker-compose.yml`, `DYNAMODB_ENDPOINT`, dev credentials in `.env`). `Shared\Persistence\Tables` creates them all the same way. Each table has the keys `PK`/`SK` and the indexes `GSI1` and `GSI2`, plus `NAME`, `EMAIL` and `PHONE` to find an aggregate. It is billed per request. It sends new items to a stream (`NEW_IMAGE`). It forgets an item once past its `expiresAt`, an epoch (TTL). On AWS, `infra/tables.tf` creates them the same way. `make db` fills them with the demonstration data (`Demo\DemoData`, written through the domain and publishing its events like a use case).

- Names are the same on every table (ADR 0190). The key attributes and the index names are those that `Tables` creates (`PK`, `SK`, `GSI1PK`…, `NAME`, `EMAIL`, `PHONE`). Item attributes are lower camelCase English (`contactForgotten`, `redacted`). Key prefixes are UPPER_SNAKE English nouns (`TO_REDACT#`). A stored enum value is its case name in lower camelCase (`videoCall`, `linkedInCompany`), except the roles' `ROLE_*` (`tests/Architecture/BackedEnumValuesTest` in the core).
- Each table follows a single-table design. Each repository documents its items:
  - `ORGANIZATION#<id>`, listed by account in `GSI1` and by holding in `GSI2`, and the links `CONTACT#<person>` / `ORGANIZATION#<id>`.
  - `EXCHANGE#<id>` sits in its contact's timeline in `GSI1` (`TIMELINE#<account>#<person>`, by its moment then its id, ADR 0074). A cancelled exchange sorts by its cancellation moment when that came first (`ExchangeKeys::positionOf()`, ADR 0112). The exchange also sits among its account's exchanges in `GSI2` (`ACCOUNT_EXCHANGES#<account>`, by its id, ADR 0099). The links `EXCHANGES#<organization>` / `EXCHANGE#<id>` sit in the organization's timeline in `GSI1` (`ORGANIZATION_TIMELINE#<organization>`, ADR 0076). While their exchange is to be redacted, they also sit in `GSI2` (`TO_REDACT#<organization>`, counted there, ADR 0079). While an exchange is planned, its link `AGENDA#<member>` / `EXCHANGE#<id>` sits in its holder's agenda, in `GSI1` under its position (ADR 0111). Whatever its status, its link `CALENDAR#<member>` / `EXCHANGE#<id>` sits in its holder's calendar (the member who planned or logged it). It sits in `GSI1` under the moment the exchange happens, cancelled or not, then its id (`ExchangeKeys::calendarPositionOf()`, ADR 0115).
  - The registry of an account's exchange fingerprints, `FINGERPRINT#<account>#<fingerprint>` / `FINGERPRINT`, holds the exchange logged under the fingerprint. It is written with the new exchange under `attribute_not_exists(PK)` (its refusal: `ExchangeNotedMeanwhile`). It is kept when the exchange is erased, with who erased it and when. It is then written whole, under `attribute_not_exists(PK) OR exchangeId = :exchange`. It is listed among its contact's fingerprints in `GSI1` (`FINGERPRINTS#<account>#<contact>`), and deleted with them when the contact is forgotten. It replaced the imported emails' `IMPORTED_EMAIL#<exchange>` marks, which are left unread on dev. An exchange imported before them reads with its email's fingerprint, which `app:keys:rebuild` registers (ADR 0154, 0166).
  - `TODO#<id>` sits among its contact's to-dos in `GSI1` (`CONTACT_TODOS#<account>#<person>`). Until it is done, it also sits among those its holder still has to do in `GSI2` (`STILL_TO_DO#<member>`). The links `TODOS#<organization>` / `TODO#<id>` go with it (ADR 0113). Done or not, its link `TODO_CALENDAR#<member>` / `TODO#<id>` sits in its holder's calendar, in `GSI1`. It sorts under its due date as written: its day, then its hour (none sorts before any), then its id (`ToDoKeys::calendarPositionOf()`, ADR 0134). A period is read between its first and last days on its reader's clock.
  - A timeline is read below the reading moment's position for the past (`Table::query()`, greatest first), and above it for what is to come (`Table::queryAfter()`, smallest first, `SortKeyRange`). A range of a partition between two sort keys is a `BETWEEN` key condition (`Table::queryBetween()`: the agenda in a window, the calendar in a period), never a filter after the query.
  - `PERSON#<id>`, listed by account and purpose.
  - `ACCOUNT#<id>`, listed with every account in `GSI1` (`ACCOUNTS`, by its id, ADR 0099), and `MEMBER#<id>`.
  - Reporting's versions, facts and figures ([below](#reporting)).
  - `OUTBOX#<commit>` / `OUTBOX`, a commit's outbox ([below](#the-outbox)).
  - `INBOX#<subscription>` / `<eventId>`, an event a subscription applied ([below](#the-inbox)).
- An aggregate is stored as a plain document (`OrganizationDocument`, `PersonDocument`, `Shared\Persistence\Documents` for the shared kernel's value objects, moments in UTC to the microsecond) and restored without its constructor by `Shared\Persistence\Hydrator`. The domain keeps its value objects and private properties (ADR 0028, 0048). A new property of an aggregate or a value object needs a change to its document.
- An organization's, an exchange's and a person's items carry a `revision`, which `save()` increments (`Shared\Persistence\Revision`). It is their facts' revision (ADR 0099), unknown to the aggregate. An item written before it reads as revision 0 until its next save. The revision is an optimistic lock (ADR 0181). While a unit is open, `Table::item()` and `Table::items()` record in it each item they first read from the table, with its revision or its absence (`RevisionLock`). The unit forgets them when its outermost level ends. `save()` puts the item with `Table::revising()`, and `remove()` deletes it with `Table::deletingRevised()`. Both do so on condition that the item is still as read: `revision = :read`; `attribute_not_exists(revision)` for an item written before revisions; `attribute_not_exists(PK)` when read absent. Outside a unit, there is no condition. The condition carries no refusal. When the item changed meanwhile, the transaction is cancelled (`ConditionFailed`). A reaction is then applied again from its reads. A command is replayed when it is an addition, and refused otherwise (`api.md`).
- A client can hold a change to the revision it read (ADR 0187). `Table::readAt()` names that revision (`RevisionRead`). `DynamoUnitOfWork::holdTo()` then makes the unit lock the first read of that item on that revision, instead of on the revision it reads (`RevisionLock::at()`, 0 being `attribute_not_exists(revision)`), until `release()`. `Shared\CommandBus` holds and releases it around the dispatch. To expose the revision with a resource, a reader calls `Table::watchRevision()` before reading the item. `Table::revisionRead()` then gives the revision of its last read (`RevisionsRead`). Only watched items are kept, for one request (`ResetInterface`): nothing is kept for the console, the worker or an unwatched read. `OrganizationRevisions`, `ExchangeRevisions` (Prospecting) and `PersonRevisions` (PersonalData) know their items' keys.
- `save()` writes the aggregate and what follows from it in the command's unit of work:
  - its email reservation (`EMAIL#...`). It is written under a condition and carries the refusal `EmailAlreadyUsed`, thrown when a second holder cancels the transaction. It is the safety net of `EmailUniqueness` (ADR 0039, 0048);
  - its SIRET reservation in its account (`SIRET#<account>#<siret>` / `SIRET`), written the same way and carrying `SiretAlreadyUsed`. It is the safety net of `SiretUniqueness`. It is released when the SIRET changes or the organization is no longer prospected (ADR 0158);
  - the reservations of each of its links and of its website domain in its account (`REFERENCE#<account>#<source>#<identifier>` / `REFERENCE`, `WEBSITE#<account>#<domain>` / `WEBSITE`), written the same way and carrying `ReferenceAlreadyUsed` and `WebsiteAlreadyUsed`. They are the safety nets of `ReferenceUniqueness` and `WebsiteUniqueness`. A dropped link is released. `app:keys:rebuild` writes them again (ADR 0160, ADR 0165);
  - the link of its resemblance key in its account (`RESEMBLANCE#<account>#<key>` / `ORGANIZATION#<id>`). Its key is kept on its item as `resemblanceKey`. The link is written when the key changes, and the former link is deleted. `remove()` deletes it, `resembling()` reads it, and `app:keys:rebuild` writes it again (ADR 0162);
  - the reservation of each current or former link a contact holds (`REFERENCE#<account>#<source>#<identifier>` / `REFERENCE`, with its `ownerId`). It is put under `attribute_not_exists(PK) OR ownerId = :owner` and carries the refusal `ReferenceTakenMeanwhile`. It is the safety net of PersonalData's `ReferenceUniqueness`. The reservations of the links the contact no longer holds, and all of them when the contact is removed, are read first. They are deleted only when the contact owns them, under `ownerId = :owner`. A reservation held by another contact (a duplicate logged before the rule) is left to that contact. A member's profile has none (ADR 0159, ADR 0165);
  - the link of a contact's resemblance key in its account (`RESEMBLANCE#<account>#<key>` / `PERSON#<id>`, in the PersonalData table, kept and written like an organization's, ADR 0170);
  - the links to its contacts.
- Lists come in slices, newest first, after a cursor (ADR 0049). The to-do lists, a handful each, are read whole (ADR 0113). A search finds an aggregate by the beginning of its name or its email (ADR 0071), and a person also by the beginning of their first name (ADR 0149). The item carries its search keys as the keys of the indexes `NAME` and `EMAIL`. A person's first name sits on an item beside it (`PERSON#<id>` / `FIRST_NAME`, with the `NAME` keys only, written and removed in the person's transaction). Its phone number in E.164 is the key of `PHONE` (ADR 0072). These indexes hold keys only (`Shared\Persistence\SearchIndexes`), and all of them are written with the item.
- `bin/console app:keys:rebuild` (`Keys\RebuildKeysCommand`, which composes the contexts) writes again the keys the tables derive from the aggregates. It can run as many times as needed. It says how many of each it wrote:
  - every organization's and every person's item, with its search keys. It also writes the link of an organization's resemblance key (ADR 0162) and of a contact's (ADR 0170), and the item of a person's first name beside it (ADR 0149). It removes the search items from before ADR 0071 (`TOKEN#...`);
  - every exchange's item, with its key by account and its link in its holder's calendar (ADR 0115);
  - every to-do's link in its holder's calendar (ADR 0134);
  - every account's item, with its key in the account list (ADR 0099);
  - the reservation of every link a contact or an organization holds. A link that two contacts logged before it was unique goes to the oldest of them (ADR 0159). Each link is recognized again by its address, and a source known since then takes over its host's links. Reservations that no link holds any more are deleted. LinkedIn profiles and pages written before links existed are taken as links, and their former reservations (`LINKEDIN#`, `LINKEDIN_PAGE#`) are deleted. When read, a link is restored as it was logged (ADR 0165).

## The unit of work

What a command writes is kept all at once or not at all (ADR 0095). So is what a reaction writes, its inbox included, in its own transaction after the command (ADR 0101). With the `sync` delivery (the tests, or an instance that names it), the reactions still join the command's unit. `Shared\Persistence\DynamoUnitOfWork` implements the core's `UnitOfWork` port. The `Transactional…Handler` decorators of each command and reaction open it (`api.md`), and so does the worker around a reaction (`contexts.md`). A gesture runs one use case (ADR 0100).

- While a unit is open, `Table::transact()` keeps its writes. A `Write` is an item put or delete, made by `putting()` and `deleting()`. It can also be a put whose condition is about the item as stored, made by `replacing()`, or an addition to an item's numbers, made by `adding()`. The unit keeps one write per item (table, PK, SK), and the last one wins. There are two exceptions: two additions are kept as one, their sum, and a `replacing()` that follows a put of its item keeps the first put's condition (ADR 0104). The outermost commit sends the writes in one `TransactWriteItems`, across the tables. Without an open unit (`Demo\DemoData`, the console's rebuild commands), a write is sent at once. `Table::batch()` is always sent at once, for a projection that can be rebuilt.
- Over 100 writes, the commit throws a `LogicException` before sending anything. A transaction is never split. Each exchange of an organization no longer prospected is erased by its own reaction (`contexts.md`), so that stopping prospecting an organization with many exchanges fits. With the `sync` delivery, where its reactions join the command, the gesture is refused beyond some thirty exchanges. The contacts an organization leaves without one are forgotten in one transaction, up to eleven writes each: the person, their email reservation, their first-name item (ADR 0149), and the reservations of their five links at most and their three former ones (ADR 0159, ADR 0165). That makes eight contacts at most, with the inbox item and the outbox item.
- A conditional write carries what to throw when its condition does not hold (`EmailAlreadyUsed::byAnother(...)`, or `AlreadyApplied` for an inbox, with the cancellation as its cause). The `CancellationReasons` name the write, and the first one carrying something wins. The handler decorators translate the refusal like any other (409). A condition carrying nothing throws `ConditionFailed`.
- A read by key (`item()`, `items()`) and a partition query (`query()`, on the table or an index) see what the open unit is about to write. An item put reads as written, a deleted item reads as absent, and an item added to reads as stored. `count()`, `keysAt()`, `keysBeginningWith()` and `scan()` read the table as it was before the commit. No command reads them.
- A `discard()` without its `commit()` abandons the whole unit until its outermost level ends. Messenger still calls an event's other handlers after one threw, and what they write is thrown away too.
- A file a use case sends to the storage stays there, unused, when that use case's unit is discarded afterwards: the storage is outside the transaction. A reaction removes a file from it once its removal is written (ADR 0101, `contexts.md`).

## The outbox

With the `outbox` delivery (`INTEGRATION_EVENTS_DELIVERY=outbox`, `api.md`), what a command or a reaction publishes is written with what it changes, all or nothing. It is published on the bus after the commit (ADR 0098), and the bus's rules hand it to the worker (`contexts.md`). The local stack and the instances deliver this way (ADR 0108). The tests stay `sync`.

- `Shared\Messaging\OutboxDelivery` keeps each event in `Shared\Messaging\CommitOutbox`, the request's state (`ResetInterface`). An event the commit publishes again is kept once (ADR 0104). Such an event has the same source, type and payload, like an aggregate's facts, published for each of its domain events. The outermost commit of `DynamoUnitOfWork` adds one item to its `TransactWriteItems`. The item goes in the table of the context the events happened in, which their source names. A discarded unit throws its events away. A transaction spans one use case, and so one context: a commit publishing in two contexts throws a `LogicException` before anything is sent. No gesture does so, as each runs one use case (ADR 0100). A commit without events writes no item. An event published outside a unit (`Demo\DemoData`) is a commit of its own.
- The item (`Shared\Persistence\OutboxItem`) has the PK `OUTBOX#<commit>` (a UUID v7) and the SK `OUTBOX`, and is put under `attribute_not_exists(PK)`. `events` holds the list of events as they travel (`{"source", "detailType", "detail"}`, `contexts.md`), as a JSON string. `occurredAt` is the commit's moment (the real clock, `app.commit_clock`). `expiresAt` is an epoch a week later, when the table forgets the item. When its events exceed 400 KB, the commit throws a `LogicException` before sending anything. It counts toward a transaction's 100 writes.
- The table's stream gives the new item to the relay (`Shared\Messaging\OutboxRelay`, `infra.md`, `local-stack.md`). The relay publishes its events on the bus through `Shared\Messaging\IntegrationEventBus` (`INTEGRATION_EVENT_BUS`, `EVENTBRIDGE_ENDPOINT` locally). It sends ten entries per `PutEvents` and under 256 KB, the smallest limits a bus accepts (ADR 0154). A refused entry fails the whole batch (`EventsNotPublished`), which the stream delivers again. The relay writes nothing. It logs each published event with its `eventId` and its correlation.
- `bin/console app:outbox:republish --since=<moment>` publishes again the items that the tables keep for the commits since that moment (`Shared\Persistence\OutboxItems`). An event may be published twice, so reactions must be idempotent.

## The inbox

A subscription whose reaction would change something if applied twice (`"inbox": true`, `config/subscriptions.json`) applies an event once (ADR 0102). `Shared\Messaging\Inbox` puts an item in the subscription's context table, in the reaction's unit of work. The item has the PK `INBOX#<subscription>`, the `eventId` as its SK, the moment as `receivedAt` (`app.commit_clock`), and an epoch 15 days later as `expiresAt`. It is put under `attribute_not_exists(SK)`. It is written first, so its failing condition wins over the others and throws `AlreadyApplied`. It counts toward a transaction's 100 writes. A reaction that fails writes none. Two subscriptions to one event each have their own.

## Reporting

The Reporting table (`<prefix>-reporting`, ADR 0103, 0104) keeps figures derived from the other contexts, which can be recalculated: no personal data, only identities and booleans.

| Item | PK | SK | Attributes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active version | `PROJECTION` | `ACTIVE` | `version` |
| Version being built | `PROJECTION` | `BUILDING` | `version`, `since` |
| Organization fact | `FACTS#<version>#<account>` | `ORGANIZATION#<id>` | `revision`, `present`, `located` |

| Contact fact | `FACTS#<version>#<account>` | `CONTACT#<id>` | `revision`, `present` |
| Account figures | `FIGURES#<version>#<account>` | `TOTAL` | `activeOrganizations`, `locatedOrganizations`, `contacts`, `exchanges` |
| Month figures | `FIGURES#<version>#<account>` | `MONTH#<YYYY-MM>` | `exchanges` |

- `DynamoFactRepository::record()` writes a fact in one transaction. It writes the fact by `replacing()`, under `attribute_not_exists(SK)`, or under `revision = :revision AND present = :present` of the fact it replaces, read beforehand with a consistent read. It also writes an `ADD` of the non-zero differences on `TOTAL`, and on the former and new `MONTH#…` when the month changes. A terminal fact gets an `expiresAt` 15 days later, longer than an event may wait (6 hours in the worker, a day of EventBridge retries, 14 days in the failures). A failing condition, or a `TransactionConflict`, cancels it (`ConditionFailed`). The worker then applies the reaction again from its reads ([contexts.md](contexts.md#the-worker)). During a rebuild, the live versions are written in the reaction's transaction.
- `DynamoProjectionVersions`: `abandonBuilding()` removes `BUILDING` in its unit. `removeAbandoned()` removes every version but the active one: what an interrupted rebuild left, and what a recording wrote in the former version after its removal. It does so with a `scan()` of `FACTS#` and `FIGURES#`, then `batch()`, once the recordings that saw the version abandoned while live have ended (ADR 0106). `startBuilding()` writes `BUILDING` in its unit. `activate()` writes `ACTIVE` and removes `BUILDING` at once (`batch()`). It then removes the former version with its facts and figures (a `scan()` of `FACTS#<version>#` and `FIGURES#<version>#`, then `batch()`).
- `bin/console app:reporting:rebuild` (`Reporting\Command\RebuildFiguresCommand`) calls `RebuildFiguresHandler` outside the command bus. Its steps commit their own units (ADR 0103). It abandons the version an interrupted rebuild was building, waits two and a half minutes for the recordings in flight, removes every version but the active one, starts the next version, waits as long again, counts every account again in it, then activates it (ADR 0106). A step cancelled by a worker recording on the same figures starts again, three times at most (ADR 0104). It writes only the Reporting table, without an outbox. `make db` runs it after the demonstration data. An instance runs it too once Reporting goes into service, after `app:keys:rebuild`. The rebuild reads the accounts from their list and the exchanges by account. Items written before those keys lack them until `app:keys:rebuild` writes them, and no one counts an account missing from the list. The rebuild says how many accounts it counted. None is no failure (a new instance has none), and `app:consistency:check` shows an account or an exchange missing from its list (`api.md`). Run it with `make console INSTANCE=<instance> CMD="app:reporting:rebuild"`, from the command line with the instance's AWS profile, outside a function's 15 minutes (`infra.md`).

It also keeps the exchanges pushed into their holders' calendars (ADR 0127), with the state of their push (ADR 0133). Only those entries follow from another context, Prospecting's exchanges. `app:consistency:check` reads the connections and the entries to report a connected member's upcoming exchange that is not synced. An entry left behind costs a withdrawal answered as done.

## Campaigns

The Campaigns table (`<prefix>-campaigns`, ADR 0138) keeps the tags members put by hand on their organizations and contacts. An account's taggings share its partition, which is read at once to suggest its tags (the range `TAGGING#`). A tagging follows from the other contexts only through its removal, once its organization is no longer prospected or its contact forgotten. `app:consistency:check` reports one left behind.

The table also keeps, next to the taggings, the facts the contacts' tags derive from (ADR 0141): identities and booleans. A fact replaces the kept one on condition that it is still the one kept (`revision = :revision AND present = :present`, or `attribute_not_exists(SK)`). A conflict is applied again from its reads. In its transaction, an organization's fact writes a link for each contact it names that the replaced fact did not, and removes the links of the contacts it no longer names. A terminal fact is forgotten 15 days later (`expiresAt`).

A contact's tags are read by its keys, never the whole partition. A `Query` reads the range of its fact and its links. Then a `BatchGetItem` reads the taggings of the contact and of the linked organizations, and those organizations' facts, which give where they are (ADR 0142). An account's geographic tags come from the range of its organizations' facts. The search for the contacts carrying every chosen tag reads the account's partition once instead (ADR 0143). It runs a `Query` of the range of the contacts' facts and their links, a `Query` of the taggings, and a `Query` of the organizations' facts, which give where they are. Each contact's tags are derived from its kept fact and filtered in the function, with nothing written for it. That is some 400 bytes per contact, so a 1 MB page holds some 2,500 contacts; an account with tens of thousands of contacts is the limit.

The account's campaigns sit next to them as well (ADR 0144), read at once through the range `CAMPAIGN#` to list them. A draft keeps no target: its target is read from the facts when the draft is shown. `bin/console app:tags:rebuild [--account=<id>]` (`Campaigns\Command\RebuildTagsCommand`) records the facts again through the same sequence. It also replaces a fact kept at the same revision (ADR 0184). Each fact goes in its own unit, without a version or a wait, starting again three times at most when a worker recording cancels it. It writes only this table, and `make db` runs it.

|---|---|---|---|
| Tagging | `ACCOUNT#<account>` | `TAGGING#<organization or contact>#<id>` | `tagging`: the account, the kind of thing it tags (`holder`), its id (`holderId`), its tags by name without their mark, in order, `createdAt`, `modifiedAt`; deleted with what it tags |
| Organization fact | `ACCOUNT#<account>` | `FACT#ORGANIZATION#<id>` | `revision`, `present`, `contacts` (the persons it names), `municipalityCode` and `country` (where it is, ADR 0142, absent from a fact recorded earlier), `expiresAt` once terminal |
| Contact fact | `ACCOUNT#<account>` | `FACT#CONTACT#<id>` | `revision`, `present`, `hasEmail`, `hasPhone`, `hasMobile`, `hasLinkedIn`, `expiresAt` once terminal |
| Campaign | `ACCOUNT#<account>` | `CAMPAIGN#<id>` | `campaign`: its identity, the account, its author (`createdBy`), `name`, `channel`, its chosen tags by name without their mark, `message`, `subject`, `status`, and `recipients`, frozen: each `contactId` with its `status` (`pending`, `processed`, `dismissed` or `erased`), whether the call to a processed one was `missed` and the `exchangeId` that processed it, the `reason` a recipient was dismissed for, whether its contact was `erased` from a started campaign (its status then `erased` when it was still to handle, kept otherwise, without its `exchangeId` or its `reason`), and when it was `processedAt`, in the order added, 500 at most. None in a draft or in a campaign saved earlier; `missed` false and no `reason` for a recipient saved earlier (ADR 0145); no `exchangeId` for one processed before ADR 0152; `erased` read from the status `erased` for one saved before it was kept. Then `createdAt`, `modifiedAt`, `startedAt`, `stoppedAt` and `endedAt` (none before, nor for a campaign saved earlier), and its `dailyCap` (none without a cap, nor for a campaign saved earlier; no `processedAt` either for a recipient processed earlier). The tags it targets are derived, never kept. Deleted when discarded, or erased by the operations |
| Link from a contact to an organization naming it | `ACCOUNT#<account>` | `FACT#CONTACT#<id>#ORGANIZATION#<organization>` | `organization`; written and deleted with the organization's fact |

## Booking

The Booking table (`<prefix>-booking`, ADR 0148, 0151) keeps each member's booking page and the link from its code to it. Only an appointment's forgetting follows from another context, Prospecting's exchange facts (ADR 0171). The consistency check reads none of the table. A page saved with a new code puts the new code's link under `attribute_not_exists(PK)` and deletes the former code's link in the same transaction. The public page is read through its code's link, then its host's page. The rate limiters of its public routes also keep their counts there, forgotten once their window has passed (ADR 0153). An appointment is kept by its identity and in its host's slot partition. The slot is put only where none exists, so that two bookings of one slot never both succeed. Both are forgotten 16 days after the booking, from the commit clock, past the last slot a link offers (ADR 0164), or removed together in one transaction once the exchange the appointment became is erased (ADR 0171).

|---|---|---|---|
| Booking page | `BOOKING_PAGE#<member>` | `BOOKING_PAGE` | `page`: its host, the account, `windows` (`weekday`, `from`, `to`, HH:MM), `timeZone`, `inactivePeriods` (`id`, `firstDay`, `lastDay`), `channels`, `open`, `code`, `createdAt`, `modifiedAt` |
| Code link | `BOOKING_CODE#<code>` | `BOOKING_CODE` | `host`: the member whose page the code leads to |
| Appointment | `APPOINTMENT#<id>` | `APPOINTMENT` | `appointment`: its id, the account, its host, `bookerId`, `slot` (a moment) and `timeZone` (the host's clock), `channel`, `firstName`, `lastName`, `phone` (E.164), `email`, `subject`, `createdAt`; `expiresAt` |
| Held slot | `SLOTS#<host>` | `SLOT#<start>` | the same `appointment`, `expiresAt`; put under `attribute_not_exists(PK)` |
| Limiter counts | `RATE_LIMIT#<limiter>-<code>` | `RATE_LIMIT` | `state`: the limiter's sliding window, serialized; `expiresAt`, the epoch at which the table forgets it |
